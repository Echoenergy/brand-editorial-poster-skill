# Brand Editorial Poster Design Skill

## Purpose

Create a coherent, reusable visual system for brand-led social-media posters. The design language combines:

**Editorial layout × Brand identity × Soft tactile materials**

The result should feel like a professionally art-directed brand communication piece—not a generic e-commerce advertisement or an overproduced AI image.

## When to use

Activate this skill when the user asks to:
- Design a social-media poster, infographic, educational long image, or poster series.
- Turn supplied copy into a branded visual layout.
- Extend an existing poster series while preserving its visual identity.
- Create AI-image prompts or art direction based on this visual system.

If the user supplies a different brand VI or reference, prioritize that supplied system over the default palette below.

## Default canvas and delivery

- Canvas: **1080 × 1920 px**
- Aspect ratio: **9:16 vertical**
- Primary use: mobile social media
- Safe margins: approximately 60–80 px left/right and 80–100 px top/bottom
- Preferred export: PNG
- Keep all essential copy and visual elements inside the safe area.
- When creating an actual file, verify its pixel dimensions before delivery.

| Hex | Role |

### Color rules
- Use blue as the dominant brand-recognition color.
- Use white/cream to carry information and create breathing room.
- Use orange sparingly for emphasis, markers, small labels, and key phrases.
- Do not distribute all palette colors equally.
- Suggested visual balance: 50–60% blue/light-blue atmosphere, 30–40% white/cream information surfaces, 5–10% orange accents. Adjust as needed for legibility and content.
- If the user supplies a different VI palette, extract and follow it rather than forcing this default.

## 2. Typography

**All Chinese content uses sans-serif typography.** This is a firm rule for this poster system.

Suggested typefaces:
- Source Han Sans / 思源黑体
- HarmonyOS Sans
- Alibaba PuHuiTi / 阿里巴巴普惠体
- MiSans
- OPPO Sans

Hierarchy:
1. Main title: large, bold sans-serif.
2. Section number: oversized, bold, often paired with a divider.
3. Subtitle: medium or semibold.
4. Card heading: bold, brand blue.
5. Body copy: regular sans-serif, dark navy/charcoal.
6. Microcopy: smaller but still readable on a phone.

Rules:
- Establish hierarchy through size, weight, spacing, and color—not decorative outlines.
- Avoid mixing many typefaces.
- Keep Chinese body text clear and readable at mobile size.
- Do not use serif Chinese fonts for body copy.
- English microcopy may use a restrained complementary typeface if useful.

## 3. Core composition

Use a flexible five-zone structure:

### A. Series label
- Place the series name near the top.
- Add a small brand-colored line, progress indicator, or restrained identifier.
- Keep it quiet and consistent across a series.

### B. Main title
- Pair an oversized number with the main title.
- A thin vertical divider can separate number and title.
- Use deep blue for the title.
- Place a short subtitle on a soft orange/cream highlight when appropriate.
- Make the title the strongest typographic element.

### C. Information cards
- Use rounded white cards as the primary content containers.
- Cards may have subtle translucency, a frosted surface, soft shadow, and gentle background bleed.
- Keep card geometry, corner radius, padding, and spacing consistent within a series.
- Common structure: left icon area; right text area.
- Use a pale-blue circular icon base and a simple blue line icon.
- Use orange highlight strokes behind section headings or small orange bullets.
- Separate modules with whitespace or a fine divider.

### D. Supporting visual
Choose one relevant supporting visual based on the topic:
- Skincare: botanical details, flowers, skincare product photography.
- Wellness: ingredients, fruits, herbs, natural objects.
- Education: restrained photography or abstract explanatory forms.
- Product education: a clear product photograph or product detail.

The supporting visual must not compete with copy. Prefer realistic photography or carefully controlled photorealistic rendering over generic 3D decoration.

### E. Footer
Use a consistent footer for a series. Default example:

**日常养护小贴士 ｜ 懂周期·更好呵护自己**

The wording may change to fit the brand or topic, but a series should keep its footer treatment consistent.

## 4. Materials, background, and lighting

Preferred materials:
- Fine paper grain
- Frosted glass
- Translucent acrylic
- Soft gradient surfaces
- Subtle embossed or tactile details
- Realistic product/ingredient photography

Lighting:
- Soft diffused light
- Consistent shadow direction
- Gentle ambient reflections
- Natural contact shadows
- Controlled highlights
- Avoid harsh specular glare, excessive bloom, plastic-looking surfaces, and floating objects.

Texture should be subtle. It should support the design rather than become the subject.

## 5. Series consistency

For a multi-poster series, preserve:
- Canvas size and aspect ratio
- Series label position
- Main title and number treatment
- Typography family and hierarchy
- Card shape, corner radius, and spacing
- Icon style and line weight
- Footer position and treatment
- Overall palette and lighting character

Allow these to change:
- Phase/topic number and title
- Copy and card height
- Topic-specific icons
- Supporting photography or decorative object
- Small theme-specific accents

**Keep the brand skeleton consistent while giving each poster its own topic-specific visual.**

Do not force every poster into identical card heights if the amount of copy differs. Adjust the layout while preserving the system.

## 6. Copy integrity and content layout

Treat user-provided copy as source material:
- Preserve all key facts, numbers, names, labels, and terminology.
- Do not silently rewrite, shorten, or invent claims.
- Keep headings and content categories as supplied unless the user asks for editing.
- If text is too long, first adjust card height, line breaks, spacing, or layout.
- If copy must be shortened to fit, ask or clearly identify the proposed edits before applying them.
- Check every Chinese character and punctuation mark in the final composition.

For content modules such as:
- 身体与情绪
- 皮肤状态
- 养生与护肤建议

Use clear headings and semantic grouping. Use bullets for multiple recommendations. Highlight only a few important phrases.

## 7. Image-generation and production workflow

### Step 1 — Brief analysis
Extract:
- Topic and audience
- Brand VI and references
- Exact title and body copy
- Number of content modules
- Key visual subject
- Intended platform and dimensions

### Step 2 — Layout planning
Determine:
- Header and title placement
- Number and height of information cards
- Icon positions
- Supporting visual position
- Footer position
- Safe margins and text density

### Step 3 — Generate visual assets
Use image generation for backgrounds, texture, supporting photography, and decorative assets. Avoid asking an image model to render long, exact Chinese paragraphs if typography accuracy matters.

### Step 4 — Typeset exact copy
For production-quality output, place Chinese text in a design/layout tool or programmatic composition workflow. This gives reliable control over font, line breaks, kerning, alignment, and copy accuracy.

### Step 5 — Refine
Check card edges, shadows, text alignment, line spacing, contrast, cropping, icon consistency, and overall visual balance.

### Step 6 — Export and verify
Export PNG at 1080 × 1920 px. Verify actual dimensions, legibility, and that no content is clipped.

## 8. Reusable image-generation prompt

Use this as an art-direction prompt. Replace bracketed fields with the actual topic. Do not rely on the image model for exact long-form Chinese typography; typeset final copy separately when accuracy is essential.

> Create a premium vertical 9:16 brand social-media infographic about **[TOPIC]**, designed for a 1080 × 1920 px mobile canvas. Use a modern editorial graphic-design approach combining brand identity, restrained tactile materials, and clear information hierarchy. Follow the supplied brand VI. If using the default palette, use deep blue `#1F3792`, primary blue `#2C59D0`, pale blue `#B2C7FF`, warm white `#FBF8F3`, and restrained orange `#E26F23`.
>
> Create a soft light-blue gradient atmosphere with subtle paper grain, gentle diffused light, and realistic soft shadows. At the top, reserve a clean area for the series label. Build a strong title zone with a large bold sans-serif number **[NUMBER]**, a thin divider, a bold Chinese sans-serif title **[TITLE]**, and a restrained warm-orange subtitle label **[SUBTITLE]**.
>
> In the main area, arrange **[NUMBER OF CARDS]** rounded white information cards with generous padding, subtle frosted surfaces, soft shadows, and consistent geometry. Each card has a pale-blue circular icon area on the left with a simple deep-blue line icon, and a clear text area on the right. Use bold deep-blue sans-serif section headings, dark navy sans-serif body text, and small orange bullets or restrained orange highlight strokes.
>
> Add one topic-relevant realistic supporting visual **[SUPPORTING VISUAL]** near the lower area without obstructing the text. Finish with a consistent, understated footer **[FOOTER]**. Keep the layout airy, balanced, mobile-readable, refined, and brand-consistent. Avoid e-commerce clutter, excessive decoration, exaggerated 3D effects, plastic textures, harsh shadows, illegible text, and random typography.

## 9. Negative constraints

Avoid:
- Traditional e-commerce promotional layouts
- Unnecessary decorative clutter
- Excessive gradients, glow, or lens effects
- Busy background textures
- Cartoonish or gratuitous 3D styling
- Serif Chinese body typography
- Tiny or overly dense body copy
- Arbitrarily arranged cards
- Inconsistent icon styles or line weights
- Conflicting shadow directions
- Altered user copy or invented claims
- Unapproved changes to brand colors
- Unnatural perspective or floating objects
- Overly synthetic AI-image aesthetics

## 10. Final quality checklist

Before delivery, verify:

- [ ] Correct brand VI and color relationships
- [ ] Chinese typography is sans-serif
- [ ] Correct canvas: 1080 × 1920 px, 9:16
- [ ] Clear title and information hierarchy
- [ ] Consistent information-card geometry
- [ ] Exact copy preserved
- [ ] No missing or duplicated text
- [ ] Icons are relevant and stylistically consistent
- [ ] Whitespace and margins are balanced
- [ ] Lighting and shadows are natural
- [ ] Materials are subtle and believable
- [ ] Mobile readability is acceptable
- [ ] Series posters share a consistent visual system
- [ ] Exported PNG dimensions are verified

## 11. Priority order

When design decisions conflict, use this priority:

1. Content accuracy
2. Brand identity
3. Information hierarchy and readability
4. Visual quality and material realism
5. Decorative details

The goal is not merely to generate a beautiful image. It is to build a recognizable, scalable, and reusable brand poster system.
